Resilient Data & AI Processing Engine (HL7 & FHIR)
Genzeon

Problem: Poll-based workflows on a legacy monolith made HL7 and FHIR processing slow to react, hard to recover and expensive to run on always-on VMs.

Approach: Architected an event-driven, Temporal-orchestrated engine that turns raw HL7 v2 and FHIR feeds into unified internal representations, resolving schema and semantic mismatches between the two. Refactored the monolith into microservices using the SAGA pattern across ingestion, enrichment and AI inference, moved workloads to AKS with KEDA autoscaling, and designed tiered archival aligned to clinical data retention.

Outcome: 50% smaller compute footprint, reactive pipelines with built-in recovery, observability and governance, and lower LLM costs through prompt caching, batching and lazy evaluation.

Unified EHR Gateway
Genzeon

Problem: Each client runs a different health system - Epic, Cerner and others - each with its own FHIR quirks, query patterns and rate limits. Integrating them one by one bloats deployments and leaves downstream applications with inconsistent data.

Approach: Built an internal FHIR server and gateway as part of the FHIR app that handles all outgoing calls to external health system endpoints. Pluggable adapters per health system normalize query patterns and responses, while request prioritization, circuit breakers and rate limiting are configurable per health system and per client deployment.

Outcome: Lean, per-client deployments customized through configuration instead of code, with downstream applications receiving consistent data regardless of the source EHR.

Real-Time Conversation Capture for EHR
Edvak Health

Problem: Providers spend hours documenting patient visits, taking attention away from the patient in the room.

Approach: Built a streaming stack that captures doctor-patient conversations live, separates speakers, identifies who is the provider and who is the patient, and generates pre-filled SOAP notes with LLMs for the provider to review. Deployed from scratch over WebSockets on Kubernetes.

Outcome: Reduced documentation burden on providers by 50% with ready-to-review SOAP notes, and improved relative speaker confusion rate by 28% with text-based role identification.

Front Desk Call Automation
Edvak Health

Problem: Clinic front desks are overloaded with routine patient calls for appointments, refills and queries.

Approach: Designed a real-time voice agent that answers calls, uses Voice Activity Detection (VAD) with semantic sentence-completion detection to know when the patient has finished speaking, understands intent with LLMs, responds through VITS based Text-to-Speech and takes actions in the clinic system. Integrated with existing phone systems through LiveKit and Twilio.

Outcome: A scalable deployment that handles patient queries over call in place of front desk staff, with natural turn-taking and fewer interruptions.
